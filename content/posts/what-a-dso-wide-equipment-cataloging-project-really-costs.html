What a DSO-Wide Equipment Cataloging Project Really Costs

UptimeHealth's own published pricing, broken down for group practices — plus the fees most quotes don't show until later.

By Pete Volk, Dental Strategy Institute | Updated November 22, 2026 | 7 min read

THE BOTTOM LINE

UptimeHealth publishes Starter and Pro pricing for practices with 11 or fewer operatories. Run their own Pro rate across a 60-location DSO and the software subscription alone comes to roughly $103,680 a year with an upfront annual payment, or $121,680 billed monthly — before the oversized-operatory fees or repair and service work, which are priced separately. None of this is a guess. It's arithmetic from their own pricing page.

Why DSOs Are Asking This Question

If you run procurement, operations, or CAPEX for a multi-location dental group, someone on your team has probably already brought UptimeHealth into a conversation. They were early to a real problem: most DSOs don't have an accurate, current record of what equipment they own, where it lives, or what condition it's in. That's a legitimate gap.

What's less often discussed until the quote arrives is how that gap gets closed. UptimeHealth's model includes physical work, not just software — its pricing page lists annual physical tagging of all your equipment with QR codes. That's a defensible way to build an initial inventory. It's also a cost structure worth understanding in full before you sign, because it behaves nothing like a typical per-seat SaaS bill.

This guide walks through what UptimeHealth publishes, what a DSO-scale rollout actually costs using their own numbers, and what to ask for when you're comparing options.

What UptimeHealth Publishes

UptimeHealth lists three pricing tiers on pricing.uptimehealth.com and uptimehealth.com (captured live November 22, 2026):

  • Starter — $118/month with one upfront annual payment ($1,416/year); about $139/month if billed monthly, since the page advertises $252 in savings. Priced for practices with 11 or fewer operatories
  • Pro — $144/month with one upfront annual payment ($1,728/year); about $169/month if billed monthly, since the page advertises $300 in savings. Same 11-operatory basis
  • Elite (Enterprise/DSO) — Custom Quote, uncapped, no published price

Two things stand out. First, both published tiers are priced per practice, not per organization, for practices with 11 or fewer operatories — the page says larger practices pay "a small fee" and doesn't state the amount. All plans are annual contracts. Second, the tier built for DSOs and enterprise groups — the one most relevant to a multi-location rollout — has no published price at all.

What a DSO-Scale Rollout Actually Costs

UptimeHealth doesn't publish an Elite rate, so there's no way to quote DSO-scale pricing precisely from the outside. But the Pro rate is published, per location, and gives a defensible floor: it's very unlikely a DSO-scale agreement settles for less than what a single practice pays a la carte. Applying the published rates across a DSO's location count shows what the software line item alone looks like, before any DSO discount or surcharge is negotiated. The first figure in each pair assumes the upfront annual payment; the second assumes monthly billing:

  • 10 locations — Starter $14,160/year upfront or $16,680 monthly; Pro $17,280 upfront or $20,280 monthly
  • 30 locations — Starter $42,480/year upfront or $50,040 monthly; Pro $51,840 upfront or $60,840 monthly
  • 60 locations — Starter $84,960/year upfront or $100,080 monthly; Pro $103,680 upfront or $121,680 monthly

That 60-location Pro figure — $103,680 a year prepaid, $121,680 billed monthly — is a software subscription only. It doesn't include the oversized-operatory fee or service work below, and the tagging question below is worth pinning down in writing.

The Costs That Aren't in the Sticker Price

  • Physical tagging. UptimeHealth lists annual physical tagging of all equipment with QR codes as a plan feature. The pricing page doesn't say who does the tagging or whether it means an in-person visit. If it does, a 60-location DSO is looking at 60 scheduled sessions every year, and each acquired location adds another. Get the answer in writing.
  • Oversized-operatory fees. Practices with more than 11 operatories pay what UptimeHealth calls "a small fee." No number is published. It's negotiated per account.
  • Repair and service work. The pricing page lists an automatic 5% discount on UptimeServices under its enterprise features, which implies standard tiers pay full rates for repair and service work. Either way, it's billed on top of the subscription.

Why the Model Costs What It Costs

None of this makes UptimeHealth's approach unreasonable. Physical tagging is a real cost if it means a person, travel time, and a scheduled block at every location, and that cost doesn't shrink as a DSO grows. The 500th location would take roughly the same hands-on time as the first. A cost structure built around field work is also hard to quote per seat the way software can be, which may be why the Elite tier has no published price. UptimeHealth doesn't say, so ask.

That's the specific question worth asking any vendor in this category before you sign: does the pricing model get cheaper per location as you grow, or does it just add another visit?

What to Ask For When You Get a Quote

  1. Ask for the all-in, per-location annual cost at your actual location count — not the published per-practice rate.
  2. Ask who does the annual physical tagging, whether it means an in-person visit, and whether onboarding is a one-time cost or a recurring one every time you acquire a new location.
  3. Ask what happens to pricing above 11 operatories, in writing, before you sign.
  4. Ask whether repair and service work is billed at standard rates, and get the rate card.
  5. Ask how the vendor's cost per location changes as your portfolio grows — a real answer should show it going down, not staying flat.
