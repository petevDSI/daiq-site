What a DSO-Wide Equipment Cataloging Project Really Costs

UptimeHealth's own published pricing, broken down for group practices — plus the fees most quotes don't show until later.

By Pete Volk, Dental Strategy Institute | August 2026 | 7 min read

THE BOTTOM LINE

UptimeHealth publishes Starter and Pro pricing capped at 11 operatories per location. Run their own Pro rate across a 60-location DSO and the software subscription alone comes to roughly $103,680 a year — before the onsite technician visits, oversized-operatory fees, or repair and service work, all billed separately. None of this is a guess. It's arithmetic from their own pricing page.

Why DSOs Are Asking This Question

If you run procurement, operations, or CAPEX for a multi-location dental group, someone on your team has probably already brought UptimeHealth into a conversation. They were early to a real problem: most DSOs don't have an accurate, current record of what equipment they own, where it lives, or what condition it's in. That's a legitimate gap.

What's less often discussed until the quote arrives is how that gap gets closed. UptimeHealth's model runs on people, not just software — a technician physically visits each practice to identify and tag every asset. That's a defensible way to build an initial inventory. It's also a cost structure worth understanding in full before you sign, because it behaves nothing like a typical per-seat SaaS bill.

This guide walks through what UptimeHealth publishes, what a DSO-scale rollout actually costs using their own numbers, and what to ask for when you're comparing options.

What UptimeHealth Publishes

UptimeHealth lists three pricing tiers on pricing.uptimehealth.com and uptimehealth.com (captured live August 22, 2026):

  • Starter — $118/month ($1,416/year), capped at 11 operatories per practice
  • Pro — $144/month ($1,728/year), capped at 11 operatories per practice
  • Elite (Enterprise/DSO) — Custom Quote, uncapped, no published price

Two things stand out. First, both published tiers are priced and capped per practice, not per organization — the fine print notes that practices over 11 operatories incur an additional, unpublished fee, negotiated individually. Second, the tier built for DSOs and enterprise groups — the one most relevant to a multi-location rollout — has no published price at all.

What a DSO-Scale Rollout Actually Costs

UptimeHealth doesn't publish an Elite rate, so there's no way to quote DSO-scale pricing precisely from the outside. But the Pro rate is published, per location, and gives a defensible floor: it's very unlikely a DSO-scale agreement settles for less than what a single practice pays a la carte. Applying that published Pro rate across a DSO's location count shows what the software line item alone looks like, before any DSO discount or surcharge is negotiated:

  • 10 locations — $14,160/year at Starter, $17,280/year at Pro
  • 30 locations — $42,480/year at Starter, $51,840/year at Pro
  • 60 locations — $84,960/year at Starter, $103,680/year at Pro

That 60-location Pro figure — $103,680 a year — is a software subscription only. It doesn't include the three cost categories below, all of which UptimeHealth bills separately.

The Costs That Aren't in the Sticker Price

  • Onsite technician visits. Every UptimeHealth onboarding path, at every published tier, includes the same step: a technician visits the practice in person to identify and tag every medical asset. For a single practice, that's one visit. For a 60-location DSO, that's 60 scheduled visits — and every newly acquired location adds another one, indefinitely.
  • Oversized-operatory fees. Practices with more than 11 operatories are charged an additional fee that UptimeHealth does not publish a number for. It's negotiated per account.
  • Repair and service work. UptimeHealth's Elite tier offers a 5% loyalty discount on UptimeServices repair and service work — language that implies standard tiers pay full, undiscounted rates for any repair or service work performed. Either way, it's billed on top of the subscription.

Why the Model Costs What It Costs

None of this makes UptimeHealth's approach unreasonable — it makes it a labor-based service wearing a software price tag. A technician visit is a real cost: a person, travel time, and a scheduled block at every single location. That cost doesn't shrink as a DSO grows. The 500th location costs roughly the same technician-hours as the first. It's also why the Elite tier has no published price — a cost structure built around field labor can't be quoted per seat the way software can.

That's the specific question worth asking any vendor in this category before you sign: does the pricing model get cheaper per location as you grow, or does it just add another visit?

What to Ask For When You Get a Quote

  1. Ask for the all-in, per-location annual cost at your actual location count — not the published per-practice rate.
  2. Ask whether onboarding is a one-time cost or a recurring one every time you acquire a new location.
  3. Ask what happens to pricing above 11 operatories, in writing, before you sign.
  4. Ask whether repair and service work is billed at standard rates, and get the rate card.
  5. Ask how the vendor's cost per location changes as your portfolio grows — a real answer should show it going down, not staying flat.
